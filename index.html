I

The passport is the easy part. The decision is where it goes wrong.

Buying a second residency is simple — which is exactly the trap. The decision touches everything at once: how your company profit and dividends are taxed, what happens around a sale or an exit, where your family can live, and how much time you can spend where. Choose the wrong option — or the right one in the wrong order — and you can create bigger problems than the one you set out to solve. And if a liquidity event is anywhere on the horizon, the window to do it properly can close long before completion.

The costly mistake is rarely the country. It's choosing before the decision has been stress-tested.

II

Most people take one of two paths. There's a better one.

Path one

Do nothing

Leave every option — and every Budget — resting on a single country, and hope the ground doesn't move under you.

Path two

Buy a passport

Rush into a programme a salesperson recommended — without testing whether it's genuinely right for you, or whether the timing even works.

The better way

Stress-test the decision first. Then build it, calmly, in the right order.

Test whether a second base makes sense at all, which one genuinely fits your position, and the sequence to put it in place — before you commit money, and before you need it. The decision comes first; the destination comes second.

III

We architect personal jurisdictions.

Successful founders diversify their investments, their currencies, sometimes even their business. Yet almost everyone leaves the thing that matters most concentrated in a single country: where they and their family are legally based — and taxed.

We don't start with a shortlist of countries. We start with the life you're actually aiming for — lifestyle, mobility, security, family, tax — then work out which jurisdictions and structures deliver it. Often that's a combination (part of the year somewhere, a second passport, the right structure), not a single country. And we're straight with you about the reasons not to choose a route as clearly as the reasons to — the best, expected and worst case, not the brochure version.
